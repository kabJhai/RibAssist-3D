# RibAssist 3D

**Biplanar rib-fracture detection, addressing, and 3D localization.**

RibAssist 3D is an end-to-end research system for detecting rib fractures in AP and lateral CT-derived projections, assigning rib levels, and localizing sufficiently confident findings on interactive 3D rib anatomy. The project combines staged failure analysis, detector retraining, frozen-policy evaluation, and a clinician-review proof-of-concept interface.

> **Sealed-test result:** under a protocol frozen before opening the 55-case test cohort, the retrained lateral detector reconstructed **15 of 601 fractures within 10 mm** at **0.436 false 3D points per case**, compared with zero reconstructions from the original detector's selected controlled-budget policy. Case-bootstrap recall was **2.50% [0.69%, 4.48%]** (excludes zero).
>
> This is a directional research proof of concept, not a diagnostic or deployable clinical system.

## What this project shows

The central question: *can fractures detected independently in AP and lateral projections be paired across views and triangulated into reliable 3D points at a pre-specified controlled false-output budget?*

The staged program (A to L3) answers it by **locating the binding constraint**:

![Locating the binding constraint](figures/fig_bottleneck.svg)

The initial failure arose from cross-view correspondence operating on weak lateral detections. Testing deterministic and learned local pair-scoring showed that neither moved the controlled-false-rate frontier on the frozen detector. This redirected the investigation toward lateral-detector quality, which turned out to be the effective lever.

**Main conclusion.** Geometry and detector localization were accurate when the correct cross-view observations were supplied. The operational failure occurred because true correspondences were buried in a large candidate graph generated by a weak lateral detector. Neither deterministic assignment nor learned local-appearance scoring produced meaningful controlled-budget recall on the frozen detections. Lateral retraining produced the first nonzero controlled-false-rate reconstructions, and the fixed-policy result transferred to the untouched sealed cohort.

![Sealed frozen-vs-L2 result](figures/main_results.png)

The 2x2 factorial isolates the gain to the **detector**, not the correspondence method: the learned appearance scorer does not beat detector-confidence at the operational budget.

![Detector x correspondence attribution](figures/fig_2x2_attribution.svg)

**Reading the 2.5%.** The low absolute recall does not indicate a deployable system; it provides a controlled proof that the detector-side intervention created reproducible, geometrically accurate 3D commitments where the original system could only abstain.

## Method (one paragraph)

A CT volume is rendered to AP and lateral orthographic projections. A per-view U-Net emits a fracture heatmap;
peaks are extracted per view and paired into a candidate graph gated by shared-axis (SI) agreement. A one-to-one
**assignment with abstention** commits a correspondence only when its cost clears a threshold (the detector must be
confident), and each committed pair is back-projected and triangulated to a 3D point. A separate addressing model
predicts each detection's rib level. Detector, extraction policy, correspondence configuration, and evaluation are
frozen before the sealed test; all provenance is hash-checked and fails closed.

## Model architecture (layer by layer)

Three trained networks. The biplanar detector is **two independent single-channel U-Nets** (one per view);
cross-view "fusion" is a geometric candidate union on the shared superior-inferior (SI) axis, not a learned
layer, so there is no fusion network to describe. The addressing network and the learned pair-scorer are the two
auxiliary models.

![Layer-by-layer model architecture](figures/fig_model_layers.svg)

### Per-view detector U-Net (base channels = 32)

Input is one 256x256 orthographic projection (single channel); output is a 256x256 fracture heatmap in [0, 1]
(sigmoid). Every `DoubleConv` is Conv 3x3 (pad 1), BatchNorm, ReLU, Conv 3x3 (pad 1), BatchNorm, ReLU.
Downsampling is 2x2 max-pool; upsampling is bilinear interpolation to the skip resolution followed by channel
concatenation.

| Stage | Operation | Output (C x H x W) | Params |
|---|---|---|---|
| Input | projection | 1 x 256 x 256 | 0 |
| `inc` | DoubleConv 1 -> 32 | 32 x 256 x 256 | 9,696 |
| `d1` | MaxPool, DoubleConv 32 -> 64 | 64 x 128 x 128 | 55,680 |
| `d2` | MaxPool, DoubleConv 64 -> 128 | 128 x 64 x 64 | 221,952 |
| `d3` (bottleneck) | MaxPool, DoubleConv 128 -> 256 | 256 x 32 x 32 | 886,272 |
| `u3` | Up + concat `d2`, DoubleConv 384 -> 128 | 128 x 64 x 64 | 590,592 |
| `u2` | Up + concat `d1`, DoubleConv 192 -> 64 | 64 x 128 x 128 | 147,840 |
| `u1` | Up + concat `inc`, DoubleConv 96 -> 32 | 32 x 256 x 256 | 37,056 |
| `outc` | Conv 1x1 32 -> 1, sigmoid | 1 x 256 x 256 | 33 |

Total: **1,949,121 parameters per view**. The deployed detector runs two independent copies (AP and lateral),
about 3.9M parameters combined. The L2 retrained lateral head has the identical shape (warm-started from the
champion lateral weights, BatchNorm, focal positive-branch weight 3.0, periodic hard-negative re-mining). A
training-only auxiliary rib-region head (a second 1x1 output conv on the shared decoder features) exists in some
checkpoints but is unused at inference; the deployed forward path returns only the fracture heatmap.

### Addressing network (rib-level assignment)

Two identical CNN streams (one per view), each 1 -> 16 -> 32 -> 64 channels over three [Conv 3x3 (pad 1),
BatchNorm, ReLU, MaxPool 2x2] blocks, then AdaptiveAvgPool to 1x1 and flatten to a 64-d embedding. The two
embeddings concatenate to 128-d and feed three linear heads. It runs on the RAW projections.

| Component | Operation | Output | Params |
|---|---|---|---|
| AP stream | 3x [Conv-BN-ReLU-MaxPool] 1 -> 16 -> 32 -> 64, AdaptiveAvgPool, Flatten | 64 | 23,520 |
| Lateral stream | same as AP stream | 64 | 23,520 |
| concat | [ap, lat] | 128 | 0 |
| side head | Linear 128 -> 1 (sigmoid) | 1 | 129 |
| rib head | Linear 128 -> 12 (softmax) | 12 | 1,548 |
| pair head | Linear 128 -> 1 (sigmoid) | 1 | 129 |

Total: **48,846 parameters**. The reported `address_score` is side confidence times rib probability.

### Learned pair-scorer (correspondence, D2b)

Used only in the 2x2 attribution, to test whether learned local appearance beats detector confidence for
cross-view matching. A shared two-layer strided-conv tower embeds each 40x40 crop (AP and lateral) to 32-d; the
head scores the pair from [ap_emb, lat_emb, ap_emb x lat_emb] plus 6 geometric and confidence scalars
(normalized |dSI|, AP score, lateral score, and the min, product, and asymmetry of the two confidences).

| Component | Operation | Output | Params |
|---|---|---|---|
| crop (per view) | 40 x 40 patch | 1 x 40 x 40 | 0 |
| tower (shared) | Conv 3x3 s2 1 -> 16, BN, ReLU; Conv 3x3 s2 16 -> 32, BN, ReLU; AdaptiveAvgPool; Linear 32 -> 32 | 32 | 5,952 |
| head | Linear (32x3 + 6 = 102) -> 64, ReLU, Dropout 0.4, Linear 64 -> 1 | 1 | 6,657 |

Total: **12,609 parameters**. At the operational budget it does not beat detector confidence, which is why the
2x2 attributes the gain to the detector, not the correspondence method.

## Status, limitations, and disclaimer

This is a **research proof of concept**, not a medical device or clinical tool. The confirmed result is directional
(2.5% recall with 6 of 55 sealed cases producing any reconstruction); the sealed cohort is small (n=55, wide CI),
and negative-scan safety is unvalidated. The binding constraint is lateral-detector availability, and the remaining
research levers are a better lateral detector and global anatomical (rib identity and ordering) correspondence, not
more local pair-scoring. **Do not use for diagnosis or patient care.** See [DISCLAIMER.md](DISCLAIMER.md).

### RibFrac (MICCAI 2020 Rib Fracture Detection and Classification Challenge)

Challenge page: [ribfrac.grand-challenge.org](https://ribfrac.grand-challenge.org/)

The RibFrac benchmark provides training, validation, and test chest-abdomen CT volumes with
fracture annotations (NIfTI). Training is split across two Zenodo parts due to upload size limits.
The challenge releases images and annotations for training and validation; test images are
released separately for online evaluation.

| Subset | Cases | Annotations | Zenodo |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Training Part 1 | 300 | Yes | [3893508](https://zenodo.org/records/3893508) |
| Training Part 2 | 120 | Yes | linked from [challenge dataset page](https://ribfrac.grand-challenge.org/dataset/) |
| Validation | 80 | Yes | [3893496](https://zenodo.org/records/3893496) |
| Test | 160 | No (images only) | [3993380](https://zenodo.org/records/3993380) |

Info CSVs (`ribfrac-train-info-*.csv`, `ribfrac-val-info.csv`) map `public_id` to fracture
`label_id` / `label_code` (0 background; 1 displaced; 2 non-displaced; 3 buckle; 4 segmental;
